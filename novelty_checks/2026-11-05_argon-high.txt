Skip connections—also known as residual connections or shortcut connections—transform a standard neural network layer from learning a direct mapping

Their effectiveness stems from an intersection of gradient calculus, loss landscape geometry, linear algebra, ensemble theory, dynamical systems, and information theory.

1.3 Eliminating Degenerate Singularities in the Parameter Space
Deep neural networks suffer from geometric singularities in their parameter manifolds (Orhan & Pitkow, 2018), where the Fisher Information Matrix becomes non-invertible (singular) and learning slows to a crawl on flat plateaus:

Elimination Singularities: If a hidden neuron's incoming weights approach zero, its outgoing weights become unidentifiable (any change to outgoing weights has zero effect on the loss), and vice versa.

Overlap Singularities: If two neurons in the same layer converge to identical incoming weight vectors, they compute redundant features and their gradients become linearly dependent.

Skip connections break the structural symmetries of the weight space that create these degenerate manifolds. Because the input 
x is unconditionally added to the output, the effective transformation Jacobian remains full-rank even when individual neurons within 
F(x) cross zero or overlap, allowing trajectories in weight space to pass right through regions that would trap a plain network on a saddle plateau.

5. Multi-Scale Feature Reuse and Spatial Preservation
5.1 Bridging the Semantic-Spatial Gap (Long-Range Skips in U-Net & FPN)
In computer vision tasks that require pixel-level accuracy—such as semantic segmentation, medical imaging, depth estimation, and image generation (Diffusion U-Nets)—networks use an encoder-decoder bottleneck architecture:

The Downsampling Dilemma: As the encoder applies strided convolutions or pooling to expand its receptive field and capture high-level semantic meaning ("what is in the image"), it irreversibly discards high-frequency spatial coordinates ("where the exact object boundary is").

Long-Range Skip Recovery: Long-range skip connections (as in U-Net and Feature Pyramid Networks) bridge across the bottleneck, linking early high-resolution encoder layers directly to corresponding late decoder layers. The decoder no longer has to hallucinate sharp edges from a compressed latent vector; it uses the upsampled deep path for semantic classification and the long-range skip path for sub-pixel spatial alignment.
