Why skip connections improve neural networks
Skip connections improve neural networks for several related, but importantly distinct, reasons. Their benefits are not reducible to the familiar statement that they help gradients flow. That is probably the best-known explanation, but it leaves out much of what makes residual architectures effective.

A skip connection changes at least four things simultaneously:

The optimization problem: how easily the network can be trained.

The propagation of information and gradients: how signals travel through depth.

The representation of functions: what transformations the architecture can express efficiently, and how it parameterizes them.

The structure of the computation: how layers cooperate, reuse features, and refine representations.

There are also secondary effects involving initialization, conditioning, regularization, robustness, transfer learning, and the interpretation of deep networks as dynamical systems.

1.3 Reduced dependence on every layer being well-behaved
In an ordinary feedforward network, an early layer's gradient depends on the product of all subsequent transformations.

A single badly conditioned layer can damage the gradient signal reaching every earlier layer.

A residual network is less dependent on the learned branches being individually well-behaved because the identity pathway bypasses them.

This is particularly relevant during early training.

At initialization:

individual transformations may not yet be useful;

the representations may be poorly calibrated;

the network may not have learned a sensible hierarchy;

gradients through the learned transformations may be noisy or poorly conditioned.

The identity pathway gives the network an alternative route for both information and gradients.

This is one reason residual architectures can support substantially greater depth than comparable plain architectures.

2.3 The identity solution is always available
The identity mapping is not merely one possible output. It is a particularly simple solution within the residual block's parameterization.

This matters when a network is made deeper.

Imagine taking a trained network and inserting additional residual blocks. If their residual branches are zero, the network's input-output function remains unchanged.

In principle, the additional blocks can subsequently learn useful transformations.

Without residual connections, inserting additional layers generally requires those layers to learn transformations that preserve the network's existing behavior. This can be considerably more difficult.

This gives residual architectures a form of depth extensibility: additional layers can be introduced without necessarily disrupting the existing computation.

5.2 Avoiding repeated reconstruction
Without skip connections, if a useful feature is lost or distorted, a later layer may need to reconstruct it from whatever information remains.

7.1 Why is this useful?
A plain sequential network has a single computational route through all layers.

A residual network implicitly contains many routes of different effective lengths.

Some routes traverse almost all blocks. Others bypass many of them.

This can provide:

short routes for gradient propagation;

multiple effective depths;

alternative ways of combining transformations;

an ensemble-like structure;

reduced dependence on every single block being useful.

This is sometimes described as an implicit ensemble of subnetworks.

That interpretation is useful, but it should not be taken too literally.

The paths share parameters, are strongly correlated, and do not generally behave like independently trained ensemble members.

The important point is that residual architecture creates a richer system of computational routes than a strictly sequential architecture.

7.2 Effective depth is not necessarily nominal depth
A residual network with 100 blocks does not force every signal to be transformed by all 100 residual branches.

Some computational contributions can bypass many of them.

Consequently, the network has a spectrum of effective path lengths.

This is one way of understanding why optimization can remain feasible even when the nominal depth becomes very large.

a more convenient way to combine identity and nonlinear components.

This distinction is essential.

The practical success of residual networks is not merely that they represent functions that other networks cannot. It is also that they make useful functions easier to reach during training.

8.4 Residual connections change the geometry of the function class
The set of functions representable by an architecture is shaped not only by its number of parameters but also by how its computations are composed.

A residual block uses addition to combine a fixed identity transformation with a learned nonlinear one.

This changes the geometry of the parameter-to-function mapping.

In particular, the network can move away from the identity function by changing the residual branch, without needing to reconstruct the identity through the learned parameters.

Consequently, the residual parameterization can have a very different structure from the corresponding plain MLP parameterization.

This is a representational and geometric advantage, not simply a gradient-flow advantage.

One qualification is important: a residual connection adds no trainable parameters by itself, but that does not mean it adds no functional capacity. A fixed architectural operation can substantially change the function class without introducing additional learned parameters.

9. Improved optimization landscape and parameterization
A residual network also changes the relationship between parameters and the function being optimized.

9.1 A useful solution may be close to initialization
When residual branches are initialized close to zero, the complete network initially approximates an identity mapping through its residual stack.

The optimization process starts near a relatively simple transformation.

The network then learns departures from that transformation.

This can be helpful because the optimization process does not need to simultaneously discover an appropriate representation and preserve the information necessary to propagate through the network.

9.2 Less pressure on early layers to immediately produce perfect features
In a plain deep network, an early layer's output must be processed successfully by every subsequent layer.

If an early representation is imperfect, later transformations may amplify or obscure its shortcomings.

Residual networks allow later blocks to make incremental corrections while preserving the incoming representation.

This can make the training process less brittle.

9.3 A better parameterization can matter independently of expressivity
Two architectures can represent exactly the same set of functions but still have different optimization behavior.

The identity pathway establishes a baseline: the existing representation is retained unless the learned branch changes it.

A residual architecture encodes the prior that useful deep transformations can often be constructed as a sequence of corrections to an existing representation.

Not necessarily.

Residual connections create a particular inductive bias. Whether that bias improves generalization is an empirical question.

18. Residual connections can improve the usefulness of very wide networks
Although skip connections are most famously associated with very deep networks, their value is not confined to depth.

A residual architecture allows the network to preserve the existing representation while adding or modifying features.

This can make it easier to use width for parallel feature construction without requiring every layer to completely reorganize the representation.

In modern architectures, residual connections therefore work together with width, normalization, attention, and other architectural choices.

They are not an alternative to width; they affect how width is used.

21. What skip connections do not guarantee
The benefits above are substantial, but it is important to separate genuine architectural properties from claims that are too strong.

Claim

Correct interpretation

Skip connections prevent vanishing gradients.

They provide an identity gradient pathway, but gradients can still vanish or explode through the complete Jacobian product.

Skip connections always improve conditioning.

They make identity-like, well-conditioned behavior easier to achieve, but the learned branches can still produce poor conditioning.

Skip connections always improve generalization.

They introduce a useful inductive bias, but generalization depends on the complete training setup.

Skip connections preserve all information.

They provide a direct contribution from the input, but addition can cause interference and information loss.

Skip connections make deeper networks strictly more expressive.

They change the function class and parameterization; the expressivity comparison depends on width, depth, activation, and parameter budget.

Skip connections make optimization easy.

They improve important aspects of optimization, but do not remove nonconvexity or guarantee convergence.

Residual branches always learn small corrections.

The architecture permits this behavior; trained residual branches can learn large transformations.

Every layer needs to be useful.

Residual architectures make it possible for some blocks to learn near-identity mappings or contribute relatively little.

The distinction between what the architecture permits and what training actually produces is particularly important.
